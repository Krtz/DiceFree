# Trimery — Village 2 design v0.3: The Roadwarden's Order

> **Current implementation:** [Trimery V1](TRIMERY_IMPLEMENTATION_V1.md) is now built into World 1. The Roadwarden remains at the ruined road, but his encounter will be accessible **from the Big City side only** and has **not** been implemented yet. The user prioritizes finishing Trimery.

Status: narrative refinement dated 2026-10-13. CONFIRMED ideas below override ambiguous v0.2 proposals. No scene, quests, enemy encounters, or boss geometry have been implemented.

## Newly confirmed by the user

- The **Ogre Roadwarden** from DiceBound Board 1 is responsible for deliberately **destroying Trimery's western road** to the Big City. This is not a coincidental landslide, meteor, accident, or an Ogre simply guarding damage someone else caused.
- The central mystery is **WHO ORDERED THE ROADWARDEN TO DESTROY IT?** The Ogre was following a command, but the identity, motive, nature and organization of the issuer remain unknown. Do not silently invent or reveal a mastermind.
- Do **not** use an ancient stone magically responding to the Echo/player as a clue or interaction. The user dislikes that gimmick. Do not build that interaction.
- Keep the Ogre recognizable to DiceBound veterans but make the drama fully understandable to people who have never played DiceBound.

## Existing confirmed Trimery/world geography

- Trimery is a working lumber exporter to the Big City, with a believable timber palisade, guards and safe core.
- Exactly three roads: **SOUTH** to Ironpine/Cornberg; **WEST** main timber-cart road to Big City, initially destroyed; **NORTH** forced route through Biome 3 humanoid quilboars, eventually reaching Big City.
- **The Big City**, not Trimery, is to be built in rings around an enormous central tree.
- Later Big City quest must enable physically restoring/opening the western road and resuming trade.
- There is an existing Ironpine approach near X223/Z149 and nearby roaming Alpha near X212/Z143; preserve guards/safety, do not use invisible walls.
- A Dice revive stone, lumber yard/sawmill, and Cornberg Runner arrival payoff remain planned, not authored.

## Optional presentation/narrative pitch (NOT yet canon)

### Visible aftermath on the western road

- Real physical destruction caused by a Roadwarden rampage: gouged wagon road, shattered paving, a wrecked timber convoy, splintered beams and a blocked traversable passage. No need to introduce an artificial river, ravine, mountain or meteor just to justify it.
- Roadwarden's attack leaves a distinct **straight-line impact/scar** across a broad stretch of road, intentionally crippling wagon transport; repairs require city assistance.
- A survivor recalls the Ogre declaring the closure in the language of a formal order rather than rage: **“By order, this passage is closed.”** The player immediately understands it was intentional, but cannot identify the issuer.
- Workers refuse to clear the road while the Roadwarden might return. Completing the later Big City quest can require confronting it and obtaining a city-backed repair crew/materials. Killing an Ogre alone never magically rebuilds a highway.

### Story structure

1. **Trimery**: villagers know the Ogre destroyed the road. Witnesses disagree about who sent it, but agree it proclaimed it was carrying out an order. Frustrated lumber merchants cannot get wagons to the Big City.
2. **Biome 3**: the player takes the dangerous north road through quilboars because western road is physically impassable. Whether someone *intended* to force that detour may become a later clue, but is not confirmed.
3. **Big City**: a city quest sends the player to make the west route safe and repairable. The restored road becomes a tangible shortcut, with resumed lumber shipments and changed NPC/world state.
4. **Later / World 2**: evidence of whoever can issue commands to a DiceBound Roadwarden may recur. Avoid explaining the Dice's deeper metaphysics in World 1 and avoid canonizing the final villain early.

### Optional callbacks, no dependency on DiceBound

- Distinctive Ogre Roadwarden silhouette, name, heavy roadblock/rampage moves and possibly an insistence on a toll.
- An evocative proclamation, seal or *noninteractive* administrative insignia on the Ogre's gear or discarded order may reappear later. It is only an ordinary clue, not a magic stone that reacts to the player.
- Recognizing this Board 1 miniboss is a bonus for DiceBound veterans. New players just see a formidable Ogre under mysterious orders.
- DiceBound story-bible notes that the Roadwarden seems to possess road authority; that background is still labelled WORKING THEORY in DiceBound and is not automatically established as the ultimate explanation in DiceFree.
