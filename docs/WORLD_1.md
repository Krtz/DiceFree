# World 1 — One-Dot Starter Face

World 1 / Face 1 is DiceFree's first detailed overworld region.

It is the **one-dot face** of the Dice and takes direct visual inspiration from DiceBound Board 1:
- `board-1-green-road.png`;
- the Normal battle environment described as a **sunlit woodland path through green hills**;
- bright, welcoming, green, road-and-forest adventure imagery.

Think **start of the adventure**: pastoral greenery, forests, small roads and a Shire-like sense of safety on the surface — while still containing mysteries that matter much later.

## Macro landmark: the Great Tree

A gigantic **Yggdrasil-scale tree** stands around the middle of World 1.

This is the one-pip landmark.

It should:
- be visible from enormous distances where terrain permits;
- orient the player naturally;
- make the Face 1 regional map read around a single central focal point;
- feel ancient enough that locals simply accept it as part of the world;
- become important later without requiring us to explain it during the opening hours.

The starter area is **not** directly underneath the tree.

From the southwest starter area, the tree should be a distant landmark generally ahead/east-northeast as the world eventually opens.

## Opening: the southwest mountain

DiceFree begins in the **southwestern part of World 1**.

A mountain near the southwest boundary/corner of the region **spits the Echo out** after the events of DiceBound / The Last Equation.

The player does not initially understand:
- why the mountain contains a route from inside the Dice;
- why the Echo emerged there;
- whether locals know anything about it;
- whether the mountain is natural.

This is intentionally a long-term mystery.

### First movement

After emerging:
1. player gets basic movement/control;
2. follows a short path away from the mountain;
3. reaches the nearby starter village.

The intro should get the player into the real world quickly rather than trapping them in a long tutorial corridor.

## Level-10 return to the mountain

At level 10, the Echo **feels an urge/call to return to the mountain that spat them out**.

The mountain is where the first advancement/blessing occurs.

There, the Echo chooses between:
- **Physically Blessed Novice**
- **Magically Touched Novice**

This means the first advancement does not require a conventional class trainer handing out a menu choice.

The mountain recognizes/responds to the Echo.

Exact ritual, visual manifestation and explanation remain open.

The mountain must remain physically reachable from the starter region after the player has explored/leveled.

## Starter settlement: small mountain village

The first settlement is a **small self-reliant mountain village** close to the emergence mountain.

It is not a capital and is not economically important to the wider world.

It exists primarily through local farming and ordinary self-sufficient life.

### Required village features

The starter village includes:
- resurrection point;
- bank;
- NPCs/quest givers;
- starter NPC crafting service;
- fields/crops;
- basic vendors/services as needed;
- later/optional travel point;
- simple low-stat training dummies where appropriate.

The bank teaches the account/Echo storage concept early.

The resurrection point should use an explicit interactable lore object appropriate to the village; exact form remains open.

### Farming identity

Fields immediately around the village grow crops.

This lets the first combat quest arise from an ordinary local problem rather than world-ending stakes.

## First quest: slimes in the crops

The first major side/tutorial quest is approximately:

> Slimes are destroying the village crops.

The player is sent into/around the crop fields to kill them.

### Progression goal

Combined:
- quest XP;
- XP from the required slimes;
- nearby natural slime kills

should bring a fresh character to roughly **level 4–5**.

Exact counts/XP are a later balance problem.

The point is that the first quest creates a meaningful early level jump and teaches:
- movement;
- targeting/basic attacks;
- taking damage;
- killing enemies;
- XP/leveling;
- returning to an NPC / quest completion.

## World 1 starter-area geography

### Southwest
- emergence mountain;
- dense mountains/terrain helping block unfinished southwest geometry;
- short path into the starter village.

### Starter village + crop fields
- compact village;
- crop fields immediately adjacent;
- weakest slimes in/near the fields.

### East / slightly north
A single main road leaves the village and travels **east, bending slightly north**.

This has two purposes:
- establishes the Green Road identity;
- keeps the player naturally looking/traveling inward rather than straight toward the southern edge of the cube face.

### South
Dense forest/terrain forms a strong natural boundary.

Do not let the player casually walk to the world edge from the first ten minutes of the game.

### Road forest
The road enters a broadly forested region.

The forest is not one corridor. It should contain:
- small clearings;
- side paths;
- openings;
- looping routes;
- pockets that get progressively more dangerous the farther the player leaves the safe road.

### Initial development blocker
For the earliest playable build, the **main road is eventually blocked by dense forest**.

That is the primary current-world expansion boundary.

As World 1 development grows, forest/terrain can be removed/reworked to extend the same road deeper into the actual face.

## Slimes as the only initial enemy family

The opening region deliberately uses **only Slimes**.

This keeps the player's first learning loop extremely readable.

All early World 1 slimes:
- are green;
- are Neutral / have no element;
- teach the most basic combat rules;
- belong recognizably to one species/family.

Different slime forms are inspired by DiceBound's Board 1–5 Slime progression.

### Crop-field Slimes — Board 1 inspiration
Closest to town / weakest:
- tiny/simple green slimes;
- equivalent visual identity to DiceBound Board 1 Slime.

### Road Slimes — Board 2 inspiration
Along the main forest road:
- larger/more developed green slimes;
- visibly stronger but still beginner-appropriate;
- inspired by DiceBound Board 2 Slime.

### Deep-forest Slimes — Board 3 inspiration
Away from the road, deeper in side paths/clearings:
- significantly stronger;
- inspired by DiceBound Board 3 Slime;
- reward curiosity but punish wandering too far too early.

### Dangerous deep pockets — Board 4 inspiration
In particularly remote clearings/paths:
- much stronger Slimes;
- likely not intended for the earliest quest loop.

### Rare elite — Board 5 inspiration
World 1 may contain a very dangerous Board-5-inspired green Slime elite.

A new player can potentially encounter/see it well before they can reasonably defeat it.

This establishes:
- fixed world difficulty;
- curiosity;
- "come back later" memory.

Exact names/levels/stat blocks are not yet set.

## First dungeon: Slime dungeon

Somewhere in the starter forest is World 1's first dungeon.

Theme: **Slimes**.

It should be physically discovered through the forest rather than selected from a menu.

The first version can be short.

### First-clear purpose

Teach:
- physical dungeon entrances;
- party/run commitment;
- wipe/reset rules;
- boss progression;
- boss XP/gold/material rewards;
- equipment only after completion;
- private loot room;
- choose one reward.

### Visible boss

The normal early dungeon ends with a **Slime boss**.

Exact boss identity/name is open.

### The boss is not the true apex

The dungeon should contain a hint that the early Slime boss is **not the real source/ruler/apex of the Slimes**.

Future possibilities:
- Mother of Slimes;
- Slime King;
- Slime Queen;
- another named primordial slime.

Do not finalize the apex identity yet.

### Hidden future route

The dungeon should also foreshadow that there is **another path** which the early player cannot normally open.

Examples:
- sealed slime membrane;
- strange plug/door;
- unreachable passage;
- suspicious wall;
- dormant mechanism;
- environmental clue.

Later in the game, some key/condition/action can open this route to a high-level secret extension / true Slime boss.

This makes the first dungeon relevant again much later.

## Starter-town long-term mystery: abandoned house

The village contains an **old abandoned house with a locked door**.

Rules:
- NPCs avoid it;
- locals do not casually use it;
- at least one NPC may mention strange noises;
- player cannot simply open it during the starting game;
- an old key or equivalent access condition is found much later elsewhere.

What is inside remains open.

This replaces the earlier burning-house concept.

The mystery should be visible from the first visit so that opening it much later feels like returning to a remembered place.

## Mountain long-term mystery

The emergence mountain itself is one of World 1's biggest long-term questions.

Early game:
- it spits out the Echo;
- it later grants/mediates the first blessing at level 10.

Late game:
- there should be reason to investigate **why** this mountain connects to the inside of the Dice.

Do not explain the mountain too early.

## Main-quest direction

World 1's main quest remains lightweight.

The starter village is mostly self-reliant and not an important trade hub.

One later village NPC should give a reason/request for the player to travel onward toward the **next settlement/town**.

That quest becomes the first explicit nudge out of the starter pocket.

Exact reason and next town are open.

## Initial blockout philosophy

The very first blockout only needs:
- mountain emergence path;
- starter village;
- crop fields;
- main road;
- forest pocket;
- several clearings/side paths;
- Slime dungeon entrance;
- dense-forest blocker at the current road endpoint.

The giant central tree can initially exist as a distant visual landmark rather than an explorable destination.

## Questions to answer next — starter village

### Layout / physical identity
1. How many buildings roughly: tiny 6–8-building hamlet, ~12–20-building village, or larger?
2. Is the village built against the mountain slope, in a small valley, or on a plateau?
3. Does a stream/river run through it or irrigate the fields?
4. What material/style are the houses: timber-and-stone, Hobbit-like cozy earthiness, alpine cabins, something stranger?
5. Is there an inn/tavern, or is this village too small for one?
6. What is the village's central gathering space — square, well, fountain, shrine, big tree, market yard?

### Resurrection / Echo weirdness
7. What physical object is the resurrection point?
8. Do villagers understand resurrection as normal, or is it something adventurers/Echoes do?
9. Does the resurrection point visibly react differently to the player because they are an Echo?

### Bank / services
10. What is the bank physically — proper banker, magical storage shrine, warehouse, strange Echo-compatible chest?
11. Why can this tiny village access the same Echo-wide bank as distant towns?
12. What is the starter crafter — blacksmith, general tinkerer, carpenter, alchemist, several NPCs?
13. What can the starter crafter actually make before later recipes appear?

### NPCs / tone
14. Who first finds/meets the Echo after the mountain?
15. Who owns the crop fields and gives the Slime quest?
16. Who is the village healer?
17. Who knows the most local folklore about the mountain?
18. Who warns the player away from the abandoned house?
19. Is there a resident kid/weirdo who claims they hear something inside the house?
20. Who eventually sends the player toward the next town?

### Village history
21. Has the emergence mountain ever "spat something out" before?
22. Does the village have a name for the mountain?
23. How old is the abandoned house, and does anyone remember its owner?
24. Are slime crop attacks normal seasonal pests, or unusually bad right now?
25. How long has this village existed under the shadow/sightline of the giant central tree?

## Production rule

Build the **smallest coherent version** of this region first and expand outward through the same geography.

Do not fully design Faces 2–6 yet.
