# World 1 — One-Dot Starter Face

This document is the active design workspace for DiceFree's first overworld region.

The one-dot face is the **first world we design in detail**. Other faces remain mostly high-level until this region proves the game's world, questing and progression structure.

## Core role

World 1 must teach the player what DiceFree is without feeling like a disposable tutorial island.

It should:
- introduce the Echo outside the Dice;
- establish that this world's geometry is normal to its inhabitants;
- introduce movement, basic combat, loot and towns naturally;
- support Novice levels 1–10;
- lead into the first advancement choice;
- contain useful reasons to return at much higher levels;
- hint at secrets/endgame content long before the player can handle them.

The region remains relevant later through hidden dungeons, crafting materials, quests, secrets and higher-level content.

## Initial playable scope

The first implementation should be **small**.

Initial slice:
- one starter town or settlement;
- one surrounding outdoor area;
- one or two roads/paths;
- a few enemy families;
- several lightweight side quests;
- one obvious main-quest direction;
- at least one camp/resurrection option;
- first training dummies;
- first physical dungeon entrance;
- one short dungeon or boss encounter;
- enough space to reach level 10 naturally without requiring grind torture.

Do not build the whole face immediately.

## Expansion model

Future portions of World 1 exist geographically from the beginning but can be inaccessible during early development.

Use believable blockers:
- tree walls/forest density;
- mountain ridges;
- blocked passes;
- cliffs;
- broken bridges;
- sealed gates;
- hazardous areas;
- story/world obstacles.

When a new chunk is developed, open or reroute the blocker.

The player should preferably see hints of future areas/landmarks before access opens.

## The one-dot map identity

World 1 corresponds to the **one-pip face** of a d6.

The exact literalness is not finalized, but the map should have one dominant visual/geographic landmark or focal point that makes the face read as "one" when viewed on the regional map/minimap.

Candidate interpretations:
- starter capital/town near the central pip;
- giant mountain/monolith/lake/crater/tree at the pip;
- a major central landmark with roads radiating around it;
- the player's emergence point itself forming the pip.

Do not decide this until the region theme is chosen.

## Starter town

The starter town should eventually provide:
- bank;
- vendors;
- healer;
- resurrection-point object;
- travel point;
- NPC gear crafter;
- basic profession/consumable service;
- quests;
- class-advancement hooks;
- low-defense training dummies.

It does not need every advanced service immediately in the vertical slice.

The starter town should contain at least one conspicuous thing the player cannot resolve yet — for example the previously brainstormed **perpetually burning house** that may hide a much later secret dungeon/boss.

## Progression

World 1's natural early progression target is Novice 1–10.

At/around level 10, advancement content should introduce:
- **Physically Blessed Novice**
- **Magically Touched Novice**

The exact advancement quest(s), NPCs and locations remain open.

Players can overlevel or wander elsewhere; this is guidance, not a hard wall.

## Enemy level philosophy

Early outdoor enemies use fixed low levels.

The same face may also visibly contain:
- dangerous caves;
- elite areas;
- inaccessible structures;
- high-level named enemies;
- secret dungeon entrances;
- endgame content.

This teaches early that the world does not scale around the player.

## Main quest philosophy

The World 1 main quest is lightweight.

Its job is to:
- establish immediate context;
- introduce the starter town;
- point toward useful local content;
- eventually suggest where the player should travel next.

It should not force every side activity or turn the first ten levels into a long cinematic corridor.

## Side quests

Early side quests should help with:
- XP;
- gold/resources;
- early equipment;
- learning services;
- local worldbuilding;
- discovering dungeon entrances;
- teaching basic elemental/defense ideas where natural.

Avoid MMO-style filler quantity. A smaller number of useful, memorable quests is preferable.

## First dungeon

The first dungeon should establish the game's run philosophy:
- physical entrance;
- clear eligibility;
- cannot join after run start;
- wipe/reset rules;
- bosses grant XP/gold/materials;
- equipment comes from successful completion;
- private loot room at the end.

It may be very short. The first dungeon does not need to prove long raid pacing.

## Return-later hooks

World 1 should deliberately contain mysteries the player cannot solve at level 1–10.

Examples:
- the burning house / Pale Devil concept;
- a sealed ruin;
- an enemy displaying ???;
- a crafting NPC requesting an impossible ingredient;
- a road leading toward a lethal subzone;
- an inaccessible landmark;
- a secret-class hint.

These are not promises of exact content; they are a design requirement that the starting region has long-term depth.

## Questions to answer next

### Theme and geography
1. What is World 1's biome/theme?
2. What is the central one-pip landmark?
3. Where does the Echo physically appear at the beginning?
4. Is the starter settlement a village, town or major city?
5. What surrounds the starter area: forest, fields, mountains, coast, ruins, etc.?
6. Which natural blockers define the initial small playable boundary?
7. What routes will eventually open as World 1 expands?

### Society
8. Who lives here?
9. What political entity controls the starter area?
10. What do locals think the Echo is?
11. How common are magic, monsters and adventurers?
12. What does the settlement trade/produce?
13. What connects it politically/economically to other faces?

### Gameplay
14. What are the first 3–5 enemy families?
15. What is the first dangerous elite/named enemy?
16. What is the first dungeon?
17. What is its boss?
18. What elements should early enemies teach?
19. What early gear families should drop here?
20. What services unlock immediately versus later?

### Quest/progression
21. What is the first main-quest objective after arrival?
22. What 4–8 side quests would naturally bring the player around the starting area?
23. How does level-10 advancement begin?
24. Where are the Physically Blessed / Magically Touched advancement paths located?
25. What points the player toward Face 2?

### Secrets/return value
26. What obvious mystery does the starter town contain?
27. What high-level threat can an early player see but not defeat?
28. What late-game dungeon/secret belongs in World 1?
29. What rare crafting/resource reason brings veterans back?
30. What secret-class condition can eventually involve World 1?

## Production rule

Design the **smallest coherent starting area first**, then expand outward.

Do not confuse "the whole first face exists in lore" with "we must build the whole first face before the game is playable."
