## Source confidence and versions
- Original link is a map overview page; it was not directly fetched in the current research session (web cache miss), so **no claim about named map locations, topology or map counts has been verified from that specific page**.
- Twilight's Eve Final map listings describe **8 classes** and **4 jobs** (job stages). Community recollections also refer to **8 class families with 16 branches**, but versions (Final/Fixxed/Evo/Reborn) have different trees; do not assert the exact tier-2/tier-3 population without reviewing the requested Final map/version.
- The user's rough "8 tier 2 and 16 tier 3" is an idea to check, **not an accepted DiceFree class count**.

## Design use
- Inspiration only, **not a copy** of copyrighted game assets, map geometry, class names or lore.
- DiceFree has its own cube-world premise and six regions, branching Novice advancement at level 10 and later resets, meaningful quests and travel, 4-player dungeons, and discovered-only codex.
- When designing post-slime-forest regions, study how world terrain and monster difficulty transition and how dungeon routes branch, but retain DiceFree-specific progression and encounters.
- No new region, enemy roster or advancement breadth is approved by this reference alone.

## User-supplied visual and play-feedback reference (2026-10-23)
Axel shared Twilight's Eve ORPG gameplay screenshots as a visual reference for
the kind of overall game feel he wants DiceFree to approach. They show:

- a full isometric play space with atmospheric fog, layered terrain, paths,
  settlements, walls, forests and memorable landmarks;
- a dense fantasy command HUD framing the game view, with a minimap, character
  portrait/status, inventory, ability buttons and clearly separated panels;
- high-contrast gold-framed panels and labels over a darker translucent ground,
  while keeping the world and actors visible behind them;
- character-entry and first-play guidance, NPC dialogue, concrete quest tasks
  and rewards, and ability tooltips with level requirements and rank scaling;
- readable health/status indicators, enemy information, area-name and travel
  transitions, and an unmistakable dungeon-completion moment.

The useful lesson is the combination: exploration and combat are presented as
one continuous ORPG adventure, and the interface explains progression and
decisions without replacing the world view. For DiceFree, translate that into
our own cube-world regions, Echo/class progression, story, art and configurable
HUD. Keep the default HUD information-rich but legible; use clear objectives and
reward feedback; preserve exploration and secret discovery; and keep combat
telegraphs distinct from decoration. Do not copy Twilight's Eve's wording,
icons, frame art, map, class roster, character options, mechanics or exact
screen layout. This reference does not supersede DiceFree's settled
androgynous single Novice presentation or other canonical design decisions.
